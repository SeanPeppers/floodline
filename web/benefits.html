Why it matters

Every hurricane season, Florida asks the same question: which streets flood first?

Floodline answers it for Key West, street by street. And it's built on a quantum algorithm that will answer it faster once the hardware catches up.

  • $13.5B federal flood insurance paid on Florida claims after Irma, Ian, Helene and Milton
  • 6,054 Key West road points scored for flood risk
  • 351× fewer simulations than standard Monte Carlo, at high precision

The idea in one line

Classical risk models get 10× more accurate by running 100× more simulations. Quantum needs about 10×.

Flood risk is a counting problem: simulate thousands of possible years and count how many flood. That's slow when you need precise answers about rare events, which is exactly what insurers and planners care about. Quantum amplitude estimation counts with quadratically fewer samples.

Who it helps

Built for the people who have to plan around flooding.

Homeowners & buyers

  • See how often your street is expected to flood this year, and in 2050 under NOAA's sea-level scenarios.
  • Check the next high tides on the live map before you park or plan.
  • Know a street's risk before you buy or renovate, not after the first storm.

Insurance companies

  • Street-level annual flood probabilities instead of one number for a whole zone.
  • Pricing rare, expensive events is a sampling problem, and high-precision sampling is exactly where quantum amplitude estimation has a proven edge.
  • Our biggest measured win is at the tightest precision, the regime that matters for tail risk.

City planners

  • Key West has a limited budget to raise roads. Floodline ranks which ones to raise first.
  • Flagler Ave tops the list: 4.7 miles, with a 99% chance its low point floods in 2050.
  • Built on 110 years of real tide-gauge data and the city's own 2024 Stormwater Master Plan.

Emergency managers

  • NOAA's storm-surge forecast runs about 630 simulations per advisory, due within an hour.
  • Inside a black-box simulator like that, our quantum method needed 1.6–3.3× fewer runs than the best classical approach.
  • Fewer runs means more room to explore storm tracks inside the same deadline.

Why quantum, honestly

We didn't just ask whether quantum wins. We measured where it does, and where it doesn't.

Where it wins

  • Most quantum demos compare against a weak baseline. We tested against the strongest classical method we could find, and still saw a 4.3× advantage once the model gets complex.
  • The advantage grows as the model adds more real-world uncertainty.

Where it doesn't, yet

  • Today's IBM hardware is roughly a million times too noisy to win. We ran it on a real IBM quantum computer anyway to show the gap.
  • On simple models, classical methods still win.
  • Floodline is built so the quantum engine plugs straight in when fault-tolerant hardware arrives.

Floodline is a research prototype and planning aid, not an official flood warning. For real-time alerts, follow NWS Key West and the City of Key West.
