Floodline

We built a compound-flood risk tool for Key West and found where quantum helps. It beats standard sampling at 4+ flood drivers and loses to the best classical method unless the simulator is a black box. That case needs hardware 100,000× cleaner than today's.

  • d ≈ 3.7–4.9 drivers where QAE starts beating Sobol QMC
  • 0.12–0.34× best classical method's queries relative to QAE, 5 drivers
  • 9.1e-9 gate error QAE's one win needs; today 0.0039

Result 1 · one street, two drivers

Quantum beats plain Monte Carlo. Sobol QMC still beats quantum.

Street: Patterson Ave, drains from the city's 2024 Stormwater Master Plan. Plain Monte Carlo needs 28 / 105 / 351× the queries of quantum amplitude estimation (QAE) at ε = 1e-3 / 3e-4 / 1e-4. Sobol QMC needs 2.3–4.0× fewer.

The curved drainage boundary left QMC's exponent unchanged: Δα = −0.04 [−0.09, +0.02].

Result 2 · five drivers

With five drivers, QAE passes Sobol QMC but never the best classical method.

Drivers: tide, 24-hour rain, 3-hour burst, sea-level rise, groundwater.

  • Sobol QMC falls behind from d ≈ 4. At 5 drivers, ε = 1e-4, it needs 2.7× QAE's queries.
  • Conditional MC + Sobol beats QAE in every one of 24 cells, at 0.12–0.34× QAE's queries with 5 drivers.
  • Exact enumeration wins on total work: 2.6e5 evaluations, no sampling error. QAE needs 9.2e4 queries of 1.5e5 CX gates each.
  • QAE leads only if the simulator is a black box, needing 1.4–2.0× fewer runs. That takes a two-qubit error near 9.1e-9; today's is 0.0039.

Verdict

QAE is quadratically faster than Monte Carlo and slower than the best classical method.

Shown

  • Quadratic edge over plain MC: α = 0.91 vs 2.01.
  • QAE passes Sobol QMC near 4 drivers.
  • A working street and city flood tool.

Not shown

  • A win over conditional MC + Sobol or enumeration.
  • Hardware speedup. Simulator only; IBM Heron noise turns the readout into a coin flip (0.50 vs ideal 0.64).
  • Wall-clock gains: a quantum query costs far more than a sample.
  • A calibrated forecast.

Methods & caveats

How we built it, and what we assumed.

Two-driver street model

Tide: 110 annual maxima from NOAA gauge 8724580, shifted to 2025 sea level, GEV fit. Rain: NOAA Atlas 14 24-hour depths, GEV fit. The Patterson Ave rule uses the Master Plan's curve number and tide runs. Annual flood chance: 0.640.

Tide and rain share a Gaussian copula with τ = 0.3, our assumption. Wahl et al. (2015) report significant surge–rain dependence at virtually all Gulf and east-coast sites; Key West appears only in their Fig. 1b, and they used daily surge and rain, not annual maxima.

Also assumed: linear storage, square-root drainage, and annual tide and rain peaks counted as one event.

Five-driver model

Adds a 3-hour burst, NOAA 2022 sea-level rise and groundwater (Master Plan range). Assumed: drainage calibration, catchment ratio, tide–rain τ = 0.2, burst ρ = 0.8. The circuit matches the closed form at 20 qubits; full-scale QAE uses the ideal law. See README_multidriver.md.

Estimators and hardware

QAE is maximum-likelihood amplitude estimation. One query is one circuit call or one classical sample. Exponents and ratios carry 95% bootstrap intervals; an interval containing 1 is a tie.

The deepest 5-driver circuit, Q256A, has 7.6e7 CX gates on 52 logical qubits. Half-time success needs gate error near 9.1e-9; IBM Heron's snapshot median is 0.0039.
