Floodline

Does quantum amplitude estimation's proven query-complexity edge over Monte Carlo survive a real, two-dimensional flood-risk problem? We built a tide + rainfall compound model from 110 years of Key West data and raced it against a fair classical control — 2-D Sobol quasi-Monte Carlo, not a method that cheats with exact CDF knowledge — instead of assuming the answer.

The question

In a given year, at today's sea level, what's the odds Key West sees a compound flood event?

Compound, not single-variable: the annual peak water level reaches NOAA's published minor flood stage and that year's heaviest 24-hour rainfall is at least a 10-year event. Tide comes from NOAA CO-OPS station 8724580 (Key West, FL), 1913–2025, shifted to 2025 sea level (2.65 mm/yr trend) and GEV-fit. Rain comes from NOAA Atlas 14's published 24-hour return-period depths for the same point, re-fit to a Gumbel distribution. A discretized Gaussian copula links them, using the sign and significance of tide–precipitation dependence that Wahl et al. 2015 (Nature Climate Change) measured directly at this Key West gauge — the exact correlation magnitude they report is a figure, not a table value, so this build uses a representative moderate positive τ = 0.3 as an explicit, flagged assumption, not their measured number.

Target probability a = 0.1090 — about a 1-in-9-year event, roughly 2.4× the tide threshold alone (0.0458, 1-in-22-yr) because rain exceedance adds real probability and the two are positively correlated. Every estimator below races to pin down this exact same number.

Why this needs a fast estimator at all: a flood-risk number that takes negligible queries to estimate to high precision is cheap to wire into every downstream decision — insurance pricing, levee design, evacuation planning — without the query cost itself becoming the bottleneck. That's the proven claim being tested here.

The claim with a proof behind it

Amplitude estimation needs O(1/ε) queries. Plain Monte Carlo needs O(1/ε²).

A 17-logical-qubit circuit loads the tide marginal, controlled-loads one of 4 regime-conditional rain distributions, and flips an objective qubit only when both thresholds hit — a discretized Rosenblatt/copula construction, no closed-form 2-D CDF handed to either estimator. Maximum-likelihood amplitude estimation (MLAE) runs at geometrically increasing Grover depths. Three estimators, exactly matched query budgets, 500 seeded trials each: quantum MLAE, plain Monte Carlo, and 2-D scrambled Sobol quasi-Monte Carlo — the honest classical control for a joint 2-D distribution (the single-variable build's 1-D stratified sampling trick doesn't generalize to it).

Sobol QMC needs roughly 3.7–4.6× fewer queries than quantum MLAE across this range — a smarter classical method still wins here. But that margin is narrower than the single-variable build's 8× gap over 1-D stratified sampling: QMC's own exponent (1.16) is visibly above 1 and above stratified sampling's 1-D exponent (0.98), the expected signature of quasi-Monte Carlo's discrepancy bound degrading with dimension. QAE's disadvantage shrinks as the problem gains a dimension — that narrowing is the finding here, not a quantum win.

The honest verdict

A real, measured speedup over plain Monte Carlo — still a loss against the best classical method.

What this demonstrates

  • The QAE vs. plain-MC quadratic query separation is real and measured on a real 2-D compound tide+rain problem, not asserted: α=1.06 vs. α=2.06.
  • 13× fewer queries than plain MC at 0.92% relative error; ~131× fewer at 0.09%.
  • Against a fair 2-D classical control (Sobol QMC, not a method that cheats with exact CDF knowledge), QAE's gap narrows from the single-variable build's 8× to 3.7–4.6× — the direction the "QAE matters in higher dimensions" argument predicts.
  • Simulator-only, ideal-circuit result — stated plainly, not hidden.

What it doesn't demonstrate

  • No advantage over the best classical method here. Sobol QMC still needs 3.7–4.6× fewer queries than MLAE at every ε tested.
  • No hardware speedup today. On IBM Heron's noise model (FakeFez), the readout is swamped by noise before any Grover step even runs: ideal P=0.109 at m=0 reads back as 0.425 noisy, on a circuit already 5.6× over its CZ-error budget.
  • Query count ≠ wall-clock time. One quantum query costs far more than one classical draw.
  • The flood model itself has uncertainty larger than the precision being chased — a sharper estimator of a doesn't make the real-world flood probability better known, and the τ=0.3 tide–rain correlation is an assumption, not Key West's measured value.
Pitch it as "we measured the provable part, against a fair classical control, on a real 2-D problem — and found the gap narrows with dimension," not as "quantum beats classical."
