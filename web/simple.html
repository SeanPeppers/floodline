The short version · about 2 minutes

Key West floods. The hard part is knowing which streets, and when.

We built a tool that answers that, then tested whether a quantum computer could answer it faster.

FGCU Quantum Hackathon 2026 · Key West, Florida

01 · How often

3 in 4

That’s the chance high tide alone pushes Key West past major flood stage during a 30-year mortgage.

Each square is one household. Shaded: sees it within 30 years. Tide gauge 8724580, 110 years of data.

02 · Why it’s hard

To get one street’s odds, you run the flood model thousands of times.

Random tide, random rain. Count how often the street goes under. More runs, sharper answer. That’s called Monte Carlo.

What that means

  • People in flood zones: street-level odds refreshed with every forecast, so you know to move the car or leave while roads are open.
  • First responders: rescue trucks, closures and shelters placed before the water, not after the 911 calls.

Honest fine print: this counts tries on an ideal, error-free quantum computer that doesn’t exist yet. Monte Carlo can also be split across many computers at once, and today’s quantum chips are far too noisy to win. See where classical still wins.

04 · How big the win is

Quantum wins big against brute force, and smaller against the best tricks.

  • 351×fewer tries than plain Monte Carlo, for a very precise answer
  • 4.3×fewer than the best normal method, once all 13 uncertain factors are in play
  • 0.65×with only 5 factors: quantum loses

We expected quantum to win everywhere. It didn’t, and we kept the losses in.

05 · The catch

No quantum computer can do this yet.

We ran a tiny version on a real IBM machine. Noise blurred the answer: 0.074 instead of 0.096. It needs chips about a million times cleaner.
