What that means

  • People in flood zones: street-level odds refreshed with every forecast, so you know to move the car or leave while roads are open.
  • First responders: rescue trucks, closures and shelters placed before the water, not after the 911 calls.

Honest fine print: this counts tries on an ideal, error-free quantum computer that doesn’t exist yet. Monte Carlo can also be split across many computers at once, and today’s quantum chips are far too noisy to win. See where classical still wins.

05 · How big the win is

Huge against brute force. Smaller against the best tricks.

  • 351×fewer tries than plain Monte Carlo, for a very precise answer
  • 4.3×fewer than the best normal method, with all 13 uncertain factors in play
  • 0.65×with only 5 factors: quantum loses

We expected quantum to win everywhere. It didn’t, and we kept the losses in.

06 · The catch

How much cleaner quantum chips need to get

1,000,000×

We ran a tiny version on a real IBM machine. Noise blurred the answer: 0.074 instead of 0.096.

07 · The bottom line

Can quantum map Key West floods faster today? No.

On the error-free machines that are coming, with every uncertainty counted, it needs 4.3× fewer runs than the best method we have. That could mean flood maps that keep up with every forecast.
